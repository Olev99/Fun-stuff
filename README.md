# Fun-stuff

- [**Zoomies! Kart Chaos**](zoomies/): a tilt-to-steer kart racer for iPhone, built with three.js. See [zoomies/README.md](zoomies/README.md) for how to play it on your phone.
- [**M4 Lydmotor**](m4-lydmotor/): an engine-sound app for an EV. It synthesises a BMW M4 CSL by default, or an Aston Martin Vanquish S, Aston Martin V12 Vantage S, Ferrari F12berlinetta, Porsche 911 GT3 (991.2), Lexus LFA or Porsche Carrera GT (picked under Innstillinger), in the browser (gear shifts, overrun burble, tunnel reverb), with an output setting tuned for the i4 M50's Harman Kardon system, from phone GPS and motion sensors, with slope compensation, reversing detection (low revs in first gear, "Rygger" in the status line) and a status line for screen lock, audio and GPS; it remembers how the phone sits in the holder (reset under Innstillinger). It has a dark carbon look with a big segmented rev counter, F1-style shift lights and an accent colour that follows the chosen car, and works upright or sideways in a holder. Open it in Safari and add it to the home screen (Share, Add to Home Screen): it then opens full screen as "Lydmotor" with its own icon (a segmented rev counter with a sound-wave glyph). Rebuild with `python3 m4-lydmotor/src/build.py`; checks: `node m4-lydmotor/src/test.js` (sound) and `node m4-lydmotor/src/sensortest.js` (sensor fusion in headless Chrome).
  - Tuned from the owner's first real drive log: in town (light throttle, easing off) the engine is about 9 dB louder on the i4 HK output and mostly tone instead of hiss, small lifts and low-rev downshifts rarely crackle, and easing off after a pull no longer shifts up and straight back down.
  - Two more cars: Ferrari 812 Superfast and Porsche 911 GT3 RS 4.0 (997). The engine ignores throttle noise from a vibrating holder (no gear hunting), and the i4 HK output has a second treble roll-off above 10 kHz.
  - Once opened online, it also starts without a network (offline cache; a new version takes over on the next launch, or after a tap on "Ny versjon klar"). It shows up as "Lydmotor" with the car's name on the lock screen and in CarPlay's Now Playing, where pause mutes the engine and play brings it back. At the default volume it plays about as loud as radio or streaming (around -14 LUFS at full throttle, -20 to -22 LUFS in town on the i4 output): quiet passages are lifted automatically and peaks stay under full scale, also in tunnels. "Veistøy" raises the volume a little with speed (up to about 2, 3.5 or 5 dB at 120 km/h, never into distortion) against road and wind noise. Volume, exhaust mode and burble are remembered per car, and all settings can be exported to and imported from a file. "Kjørelogg" can record drives (speed, acceleration, throttle, brake, gear, rpm; position only if ticked) for tuning the sensor logic; download or share the log as a JSON file and analyse it with `node m4-lydmotor/src/loganalyze.js <file>`.
  - The app measures how late the phone's GPS speed arrives (about 0.11 s on the owner's iPhone, not the 1 s it used to assume), keeps the estimate with the holder calibration and shows it in the drive panel ("GPS-forsinkelse"); the throttle estimate no longer hovers at the engine's lift threshold in town (fewer needless gear changes).
